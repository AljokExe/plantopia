| Humidity Requirement | 80%, Ventilation | Clemson: "above 60%" (no 60% option, so 80%). NC State: "If the humidity is too low, the traps or pitchers will not develop properly or will die back", "Poor air circulation can cause fungal diseases" |
| Soil Type | Excellent Drainage, Slightly Acid | NC State: "Good Drainage", "Acid (<6.0)", "moist but well-drained with an acidic pH". RHS: "Moist but well–drained". Not "Allow soil to dry": Clemson says "do not allow plants to dry out" |
| Special Care Instructions | Water only with rainwater or distilled water. Keep the mix moist and never let it dry out, but do not let the pot stand in water. | NC State: not able to tolerate "watering with tap water", "Distilled water or rainwater should be used". Clemson: "Keep moist; do not allow plants to dry out". RHS: "do not allow to sit in water" |

**Potting mix:** Clemson: "60% sphagnum moss, 20% perlite, 20% orchid bark", "a light, low-nutrient potting medium". RHS: "loose organic medium, such as a mixture of bark, sustainably sourced sphagnum-type moss and charcoal".

**Feeding:** NC State: feed with insects, or "an organic fertilizer during the growing season". "They do not digest protein and should not be feed meat." Clemson: dilute fertilizer to ¼ strength or less and apply it "to the leaves and open pitchers".

**Pruning and pests:** RHS: "Trim out spent pitchers", "May be susceptible to scale insects". NC State: "Monitor for aphids, mealybugs, thrips, and scale insects."

**Safety:** Clemson: "Pet Safe: Yes".

## Humidity methods

- **Pebble tray:** sources disagree. Nebraska Extension: "As water evaporates from the pebble tray, it will increase humidity around the plant's foliage." Penn State: "the evaporated air diffuses into the entire room, making almost no overall impact". If used, keep the water "below the base of the plant's container"; "The plant should never sit in water" (Nebraska).
- **Better:** a cloche or terrarium "recycles humidity in the plant's immediate surroundings" (Penn State), with some airflow (NC State). A portable humidifier also helps (Nebraska, Penn State).
- **Do not mist:** misting "is not an effective solution" (Nebraska).
